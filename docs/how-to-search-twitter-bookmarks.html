How to Search Your Twitter Bookmarks

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Saved a great tweet weeks ago and can't find it? You're not alone. Here's why Twitter/X bookmark search is broken and what you can do about it.

The Problem: X Doesn't Let You Search Bookmarks

X's built-in bookmarks have no search function. The only way to find a saved tweet is to scroll through your entire bookmark list until you see it. If you've saved 500 tweets, you might scroll through all 500.

The X search bar searches the entire platform — public tweets from everyone — not your personal bookmarks. There's no way to search only the tweets you've saved.

⚠️ Why This Matters

You saved a tweet because it was useful. But if you can't find it later, saving it was pointless. The lack of bookmark search makes X bookmarks almost useless for anyone who saves more than a handful of tweets.

The Solution: Use a Tool with Full-Text Search

Third-party bookmark managers provide the search function X doesn't. Here's how to get searchable Twitter bookmarks:

Step 1: Install bookmarker

Download the bookmarker app from the iOS App Store. It's a native iOS app that integrates with the share sheet and provides full-text search.

Step 2: Save Tweets Through bookmarker

When you see a tweet you want to save, tap the share button and select "bookmarker." bookmarker saves the tweet and fetches the full text so it's searchable later.

Step 3: Search Anytime

Open bookmarker and type any keyword, phrase, or author name into the search bar. You'll see every saved tweet that matches. The search looks through the full text of the tweet, not just the username.

💡 How Full-Text Search Works

bookmarker fetches the content of every tweet when you save it. This means you can search for words that appear in the tweet text, even if they're not in the username or hashtags. Example: search "sourdough starter" and find every recipe thread you saved that mentions sourdough, even if the author never used that exact hashtag.

Alternative: Export to a Spreadsheet

Some people export their X bookmarks to a CSV file and use spreadsheet search (Ctrl+F in Excel or Google Sheets). This works but it's manual: you have to export periodically, and the export is a snapshot (it doesn't update when you save new tweets).

How to Export X Bookmarks

  1. Go to X Settings → Your Account → Download an archive of your data
  2. Wait for X to prepare your archive (can take 24 hours)
  3. Download the archive and extract the ZIP file
  4. Find the bookmarks file (usually in a JSON or CSV format)
  5. Open it in a spreadsheet and use Ctrl+F to search

This method is slow, requires re-exporting regularly, and doesn't work on mobile. It's better than nothing, but it's not a real solution.

Why Not Just Remember the Author?

If you remember who posted the tweet, you can search X for that author and scroll through their timeline. But this only works if:

  • You remember the author's username
  • The author hasn't deleted the tweet
  • The author hasn't posted hundreds of tweets since then

Most of the time, you don't remember the author — you just remember the content. That's why full-text search matters.

What About X's Advanced Search?

X's advanced search (search operators like "from:username" and "since:2024-01-01") searches all of X, not your bookmarks. You can narrow results by date or author, but you're still searching the entire platform, not just your saved tweets.

Search Tips for bookmarker

Once you have searchable bookmarks, here's how to get the most out of search:

1. Search by Topic

Type a broad keyword like "sourdough" or "NextJS" to see all saved tweets about that topic.

2. Search by Phrase

Use quotes to search for exact phrases: "cold brew recipe" finds only tweets with that exact phrase.

3. Filter by Platform

If you save content from X, Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube in bookmarker, you can filter search results to show only tweets (or only Instagram posts, etc.).

4. Search by Author

Type an author's name or username to see all tweets you've saved from them.

The Bottom Line

X does not provide bookmark search, and there's no native way to search your saved tweets. The practical solution is to use a third-party tool like bookmarker that provides full-text search. Save tweets through bookmarker, and you can search them by keyword, phrase, or author anytime.

Without search, bookmarks are just a pile. With search, they're a useful reference library.
