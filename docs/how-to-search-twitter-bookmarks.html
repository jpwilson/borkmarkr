How to search your X (Twitter) bookmarks

Last updated: 21 October 2026

Saved a good post weeks ago and cannot find it? The reason is that X's search box searches X, not your saves, and scrolling a long bookmark list is not searching. This page covers the two methods that actually work — exporting your archive, and keeping saves in a library built to be searched — and is specific about what each one can and cannot match.

X's own bookmark features change, and some of them (folders, for example) have been limited to paid X Premium tiers. Check X's current help pages before assuming what your account can do. The method below does not depend on which tier you are on.

Why scrolling fails

A bookmark list is ordered by when you saved. You remember what something was about, who posted it, or roughly what it looked like — almost never the date. So the one axis the list gives you is the one axis you cannot use, and with a few hundred saves the only honest strategy left is luck.

Method 1: export your X archive and search the file

  1. Open X → Settings → Your account → Download an archive of your data.
  2. Wait for X to prepare it — this can take a day.
  3. Download the archive and unzip it.
  4. Open the bookmarks data file and search it in a spreadsheet or text editor.

What it is good for: a one-off hunt, and it costs nothing. What it is bad for: it is a snapshot that never updates, it is awkward on a phone, and you have to re-export to catch anything new.

Method 2: keep your saves in a library you can search

The method with no maintenance: save through something that records the useful fields at save time, so finding is a search rather than an excavation. bookmarker is free, works from the X share sheet, and holds your Instagram, TikTok and YouTube saves in the same place.

  1. Get bookmarker. Install bookmarker free from the App Store (iOS 17 or later) or open the web app at bookmarker.lol.
  2. Save X posts through it. In X, tap Share, then “Share via…”, then bookmarker. The link, the title where X publishes one, the account and the source app are recorded, and the save is filed into a topic.
  3. Import the bookmarks you already have. Request your X archive under X Settings → Your account → Download an archive of your data, then import the file so your existing saves become searchable too.
  4. Search your library. Type a word into the search field. bookmarker matches the title, the account that posted it, your tags, your notes, the topic and subtopic, the source app, the link, and any description text the page published.
  5. Narrow it when a word is too common. Tap the Topics, Subtopics or Tags chips to restrict a search to those fields, or filter the results by source app to see only X.

Exactly what bookmarker's search matches

Worth spelling out, because "full-text search" gets claimed loosely in this category. A bookmarker search looks at:

  • the title of the save;
  • the account or handle that posted it;
  • your tags;
  • your notes;
  • the topic and subtopic it was filed into;
  • the source app;
  • the link itself;
  • and whatever post text bookmarker holds — the description a page published, which Instagram, TikTok and X usually do not provide, or the full post body when the save arrived in an X data export.

What it does not do is open the page behind the link. The body of an article, a PDF or a video transcript is never fetched, so none of it is searchable, and there is no quoted-phrase or operator syntax: typing several words means "every one of these must appear somewhere". If you need search that reaches inside saved page content, Raindrop.io does that and we say so on the comparison page. If you want a full, lasting copy of your X posts rather than the text an export happens to carry, Twillot archives them.

Getting more out of a search

Search the word you would use for the subject

Because every save is filed into a topic and subtopic, the subject word usually hits even when the title is useless — searching "mobility" finds things filed under Fitness › Mobility whose titles never said it.

Scope the search when a word is everywhere

A query like "running" matches eleven titles as loudly as the topic you actually filed things under. Tapping the Topics, Subtopics or Tags chips restricts the match to those fields, which is usually the fix.

Filter by the app it came from

If you know you saved it on X, filter the results by source and the list collapses to a handful.

Give future-you something to search for

A tag or a one-line dated note at save time is the single highest-leverage habit here — those are your words, so they are the words you will search for.

Why author search is not the answer

Searching X for the person who posted it only works if you remember who they were, they have not deleted the post, and they have not posted a thousand times since. Most of the time you remember the content, not the author. X's advanced search operators have the same limit: they search all of X, not your saves.
