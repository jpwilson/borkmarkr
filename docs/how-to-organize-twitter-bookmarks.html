How to Organize Your Twitter Bookmarks

Last updated: October 19, 2026

If you've saved hundreds of tweets and can't find any of them, you're not alone. Twitter/X bookmarks are notoriously hard to organize. Here's how to actually get your bookmarks under control.

The Problem: X Doesn't Let You Organize Bookmarks

X's built-in bookmark feature doesn't support folders, tags, or categories. Every bookmark goes into one long chronological list. If you've saved 500 tweets, you have to scroll through all 500 to find anything.

There's no native way to organize X bookmarks into topics like "coding threads," "recipe tweets," or "fitness advice." X just gives you a pile.

The Solution: Automatic Categorization

The most effective way to organize Twitter bookmarks is to use a tool that automatically categorizes tweets by topic as you save them. Here's how it works:

Step 1: Install bookmarker

Download the bookmarker app from the iOS App Store. It's a native iOS app that integrates with the share sheet.

Step 2: Save a Tweet

When you see a tweet you want to save, tap the share button (the icon with the arrow pointing up) and select "bookmarker" from the share sheet.

Step 3: Let It Categorize

bookmarker automatically reads the tweet and categorizes it by topic: Technology, Food & Drink, Fitness, Business, etc. You don't have to pick a category — it happens automatically when you save.

Step 4: Browse by Topic

Open bookmarker and browse by topic. All your coding tweets are together. All your recipe tweets are together. All your fitness threads are together. No manual filing required.

💡 Pro Tip: Manual Tags for Specific Projects

Automatic categorization handles most organization. For specific projects or collections, you can add manual tags. Example: tag tweets about a specific conference with "devcon2026" so you can find all of them instantly.

Alternative: Manual Note-Taking

Some people screenshot tweets and paste them into notes apps like Apple Notes, Notion, or Obsidian. This works but it's slow, and you lose the ability to click through to the original tweet or see replies.

Why Not Use X Lists?

X Lists are for following specific accounts, not for organizing saved tweets. You can't add a tweet to a List after you've seen it — Lists are about whose tweets you see in your timeline, not about organizing content you've already saved.

Organizing by Time vs. Topic

X's bookmark list is chronological: newest first. This works if you saved something yesterday and want to find it today. It doesn't work if you saved something three months ago.

Organization by topic is more useful long-term. You don't remember when you saved a great coding thread — you remember it was about coding. Topic-based organization matches how you actually look for saved content.

Organizing Multiple Platforms

If you save content from Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube too, you end up with four separate bookmark lists. bookmarker works across all of them, so your Instagram recipe reel and your X recipe thread both go into the same "Food & Drink" category.

The Bottom Line

X doesn't let you organize bookmarks natively. The most practical solution is automatic categorization with a tool like bookmarker. It files tweets by topic as you save them, so you can actually find them later without scrolling through hundreds of bookmarks.
