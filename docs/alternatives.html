Best bookmark managers for saved posts and X bookmarks

Last updated: 21 October 2026 · written by the team that makes bookmarker

If you save a lot on X, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube and can never find any of it again, there are about five serious options, and they are built for genuinely different jobs. This page says what each one is for, where it beats the others, and which parts of the problem it does not touch. We make bookmarker, so treat the summary of our own product as the one you should check hardest.

Competitor details below are from each product's own website, checked on 21 October 2026, and they change. Follow the links and confirm anything that matters to you. Where a rival is better at something, it says so.

bookmarker

What it is: a free bookmark manager for iPhone and the web that collects everything you save across apps into one library and files each save into a topic and subtopic automatically — 50 topics, 612 subtopics. You save with the iOS share sheet, by pasting a link, or by importing your X, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube or browser-bookmark export. You can then browse by topic or by source app, search, add dated notes, and share a set of saves as a link.

Strongest at: the cross-app problem. It is the only tool here whose whole premise is that your Instagram reel and your X thread about the same marathon belong in the same place. Filing is automatic, so a library of a few thousand saves is usable without you organising anything.

Weakest at: depth per platform and depth per page. It stores links, not copies, so it cannot show you a post that has been deleted, and search covers only the text that came with a save — never the article, PDF or transcript behind the link. Thumbnails and titles are frequently missing for Instagram, TikTok and X saves because those platforms do not publish metadata to unauthenticated requests. There is no Android app and no browser extension, though the web app runs in an Android or desktop browser. It cannot reach into X and pull your existing bookmark list down — for that you import X's data export.

Price: free on the App Store; first 20 saves with no account, then a free email sign-in for an unlimited, backed-up library. Full FAQ.

Raindrop.io

What it is: a mature, all-purpose bookmark manager. Per raindrop.io, it runs as a web app, browser extensions and apps across platforms, and organises saves into nestable collections with tags and filters. It also offers highlights and annotations, duplicate and broken-link detection, several view modes, integrations, archived copies of saved pages, and full-text search across the content of saved pages, PDFs and YouTube videos.

Stronger than bookmarker at: almost everything to do with web pages. If what you save is mostly articles and research, Raindrop is the better tool — real full-text search inside saved content, and a permanent archived copy so a dead link still has something behind it. Raindrop also offers native Android and desktop apps and browser extensions; bookmarker offers an iPhone app and a web app usable in desktop and Android browsers.

Where bookmarker differs: Raindrop is browser-first and you choose the collection; bookmarker is phone-share-sheet-first and files every save into a topic for you. If your saving happens inside Instagram and TikTok rather than in a browser, that difference matters more than the feature lists do.

Twillot

What it is: per twillot.com, a browser extension that turns your X (Twitter) bookmarks into a searchable library — it syncs your bookmarks, likes, posts and replies into an archive held in your browser, with folders, AI organising, search, media downloads and export, and it keeps posts after they are deleted.

Stronger than bookmarker at: X, by a wide margin. It reaches your existing X bookmark history directly instead of asking you to import a data export, it keeps copies so deleted posts survive, and it exports your archive. If your problem is specifically "I have 4,000 X bookmarks", start there.

Where bookmarker differs: Twillot is X-only and lives in a desktop browser. bookmarker covers Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and the open web as well, and works from the phone you were already holding.

mymind

What it is: per mymind.com, a private visual library for notes, bookmarks, inspiration, articles and images — "remember everything, organize nothing". You save with a click and its AI handles the organising, so its home page says you need not file, label or even tag anything. Organising is optional rather than absent: mymind's own guide to Spaces describes adding your own tags, saving a search as a Smart Space that fills itself, and right-clicking an item to add it to a Space by hand. It has iPhone and Android apps plus Chrome, Edge and Safari extensions, and explicitly has no social features, no collaboration and no ads.

Stronger than bookmarker at: visual material and craft. If you are collecting images, palettes, type and product shots, mymind's visual library is a nicer place to look at them than a list of links, and it has a native Android app and browser extensions where bookmarker has neither.

Where bookmarker differs: bookmarker leads with the structure — a visible topic tree you browse and reassign, with the source app as a dimension of its own — where mymind leads with search over a library that organised itself. bookmarker is built around posts from social apps, and it is free.

The bookmarks built into X, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube

What they are: free, instant, and already on your phone. For saving one thing you will look at tomorrow, nothing beats them.

Where they stop: each one is a separate list inside a separate app, so what you save is split four or five ways. Most of them show you no title worth reading, no topic to browse by, and no record of what you have actually gone back to. Feature availability varies and changes — X, for instance, has added bookmark folders for subscribers — so check the current help pages of each app rather than trusting an older comparison.

What about Pocket and Instapaper?

Pocket, the best-known read-it-later app, has shut down: Mozilla's notice on getpocket.com says the Pocket web, Android, iOS and macOS apps and the browser extensions have been phased out, so it is no longer an option. Instapaper is still a good read-later app for long articles, but it is built for reading text, not for keeping track of reels, clips and threads — which is the problem this page is about.

How to choose

  • Most of what you save is in phone apps, across several of them → bookmarker.
  • Most of what you save is web articles and research → Raindrop.io.
  • You only care about X, and you want your existing bookmark history → Twillot.
  • You collect images, want it to look beautiful, or need Android or a browser extension → mymind.
  • You save a handful of things a month → the built-in bookmarks are fine.

Plenty of people run two: a web-first manager for articles and bookmarker for everything the phone throws at them. The workflows barely overlap.
