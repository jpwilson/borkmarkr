bookmarker vs Raindrop.io

Last updated: 21 October 2026

Both are bookmark managers, and they are built for different halves of the problem. Raindrop.io is a mature, cross-platform manager for web pages, articles and research that you save from a browser. bookmarker is a free iPhone and web library for the posts you save inside Instagram, X, TikTok and YouTube, filed into topics automatically. If your saving happens in a browser, Raindrop is probably the better answer; if it happens in phone apps, bookmarker is.

Competitor details here come from the other product's own website, checked on 21 October 2026, and they change. Follow the links and confirm anything that matters to you. Where the other tool is better, it says so.

What Raindrop.io does, in its own terms

Per raindrop.io, Raindrop runs as a web app, browser extensions and apps across platforms, and organises saves into nestable collections with tags and filters. It finds duplicates and broken links, offers highlights and annotations, several view modes (grid, headlines, masonry, list), integrations and an API, sharing and permissions for collaborators, and backups to Dropbox or Google Drive. Two things stand out as genuinely strong: it keeps an archived copy of saved web pages, so a page that is taken down still has something behind it; and its full-text search covers the entire content of every saved page, PDF and YouTube video, not just titles and tags. It has a free plan and a paid subscription.

What bookmarker does

bookmarker starts from the phone. You tap Share in any app and pick bookmarker, or paste a link, or import the data export from X, Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. Every save lands in a topic and subtopic automatically — 50 topics, 612 subtopics, all overridable — and the app it came from is recorded, so you can browse by topic ("everything about running, wherever it came from") or by source ("everything I kept from TikTok"), with each filtering the other. You can add a dated note to any save, bookmarker counts how often you reopen something, and you can turn a set of saves into a shareable page at bookmarker.lol/c/… with an optional expiry.

Where Raindrop.io is better

  • Search depth. Raindrop searches inside the content of what you saved. bookmarker does not — see the note below.
  • Archiving. Raindrop keeps copies of saved pages. bookmarker stores links only, so a deleted post is simply gone.
  • Platform coverage. Raindrop has browser extensions and runs on Android, Windows and macOS. bookmarker is an iPhone app plus a web app, with no browser extension and no Android app.
  • Manual structure and teamwork. Nestable collections, permissions, shared editing, an API.
  • Maturity. It has been around far longer and has the feature surface to show for it.

One thing to be clear about, because it is easy to oversell: bookmarker does not index the inside of the posts you save. A search matches the title, the account that posted it, your tags, your notes, the topic and subtopic, the source app, the link, and any description text the page published. You can narrow it to topics, subtopics or tags. Several words mean "all of these must appear"; there is no quoted-phrase operator.

Where bookmarker is better

  • Saving from social apps. bookmarker is designed for the share sheet inside Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and X, and it labels every save with the app it came from.
  • Filing you do not have to do. Raindrop asks which collection; bookmarker picks a topic and subtopic and lets you correct it. With a few thousand saves, that difference is the whole product.
  • Two browse axes that cross. Topic and source app, each filtering the other.
  • Dated notes and revisit counts. Why you kept something, and whether you ever went back.
  • Price. bookmarker is free, including the unlimited, backed-up library after a free email sign-in.

Can you use both?

Yes, and it is a sensible setup: Raindrop for articles and research you save in a browser, bookmarker for the reels, clips and threads you save on your phone. The workflows barely touch — one is browser-first, the other is share-sheet-first.

The bottom line

Choose Raindrop.io if most of what you keep is web pages, you want them searchable to the word and archived, and you need Android, Windows or a browser extension. Choose bookmarker if most of what you keep is posts from social apps, you want them filed by topic without doing it yourself, and you want it free.
