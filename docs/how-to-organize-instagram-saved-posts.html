How to organise your Instagram saved posts

Last updated: 6 November 2026

If you tap the bookmark icon on Instagram several times a day, your Saved tab is probably hundreds of reels and posts deep, and finding the one recipe or workout you meant to come back to means scrolling. This page covers what Instagram's own collections do well, where they stop, and a method that files each save by topic as you make it — including the posts you saved years ago.

Instagram changes its menus often. The paths below were right when this page was updated; if a label has moved, the same option is usually one screen away.

What Instagram gives you: Saved and collections

Every post you bookmark goes into Saved (your profile → the menu → Saved), ordered by when you saved it. You can create collections and add posts to them, either as you save or afterwards. For a handful of broad buckets — "Recipes", "Trips" — that works, it costs nothing, and the posts stay inside Instagram where they play best.

Where it stops:

  • Every post is a manual decision. Collections only fill when you file into them, so most people stop after the first week and the "All posts" grid keeps growing.
  • It only holds Instagram. The TikTok, YouTube and X saves about the same subject live in three other apps.
  • It is built for scrolling, not finding. The grid shows covers, and you remember what a post was about, not when you saved it.

The method: file by topic as you save

The approach with the least ongoing effort is to save through something that suggests the subject at save time, so the organising has already happened when you come looking. bookmarker is free, takes posts from Instagram's share button, and keeps them next to everything you save from other apps.

  1. Get bookmarker. Install bookmarker free from the App Store (iOS 17 or later), or open the web app at bookmarker.lol. Your first 20 saves work with no account.
  2. Share a post from Instagram. On the post or reel, tap the Share (paper plane) button and choose the option that opens the iPhone share sheet, then pick bookmarker. Or tap Copy link and paste it into bookmarker. Pin bookmarker to the front of the share sheet once — More → Edit → add to Favorites — and it is the first icon from then on.
  3. File it in one tap. The save happens at once. A dial then offers the seven likeliest topics with the best guess in the middle: tap to file it, turn the dial to pick another, or leave it and the guess stands. Signed in, an AI model also suggests a topic and subtopic.
  4. Bring in the posts you already saved. Request your Instagram data (Settings → Accounts Centre → Your information and permissions → Download your information, in JSON format), then drop the .zip into the web app's importer, or import the saved_posts.json inside it on iPhone.
  5. Sort the imported pile. Imported Instagram saves arrive with the account and the date but no caption, so many need a topic. Open Sort your borks and file them one card at a time; bookmarker remembers where you put each account, so the next post from them is suggested first.

Importing years of saved posts

Instagram will hand you a copy of your data. Ask for it in JSON, not HTML: the email can take anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of days, and the download link expires after a few days, so grab it when it lands. In the bookmarker web app on a computer you can drop the whole .zip in without unzipping it; the importer finds saved_posts.json (and your collections) inside. On iPhone, unzip it and import saved_posts.json from the app.

Be clear about what the export contains: the link to each post, the account that posted it and the date you saved it. It does not contain captions or images, and Instagram does not publish them to an app that is not logged in, so imported posts arrive as plain cards with the account name. That is why the last step above matters: Sort your borks shows anything without a topic one card at a time, and because bookmarker learns where you file each account, a creator you have filed three times is suggested first from then on.

Finding a saved post again

Once a post is in bookmarker you can find it by:

  • the topic and subtopic it is filed under — Recipes › Meal prep, Fitness › Mobility;
  • the account that posted it;
  • any tag or dated note you added — the single best habit, because those are your own words;
  • the source app, to see only what came from Instagram;
  • and the link and title.

Search does not see inside the post. For Instagram that matters more than for other apps: there is usually no caption to search, so the topic, the account and your own tags do the work. If you want the words of a reel to be searchable, add a short note when you save it.

Topics beat collections across apps

The reason to organise outside Instagram is that your interests are not split by app. A mobility routine saved on Instagram, a stretching video on YouTube and a thread on X all belong in Fitness › Mobility. In bookmarker they sit together, each still labelled with the app it came from, and opening one sends you straight back to the original post in Instagram.

What this will not do

bookmarker stores links, not copies. If a creator deletes a post or makes their account private, the link stops working — it is a library, not an archive. It does not download reels, and it cannot reach into Instagram and pull your Saved list down by itself; the data export is the way to bring that across. There is no Android app, though the web app runs in any browser.
