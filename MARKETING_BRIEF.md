# borkmarkr — brief for marketing

Written for the parallel session working on marketing material. This is JP's
positioning in his own framing, plus what's actually true about the product
today so nothing gets promised that doesn't exist.

---

## The one-line framing (JP's, and it's the right one)

> "The whole thing is a **post link management system** if you think about it."

Instagram has nothing to do with YouTube Shorts. Shorts has nothing to do with
Threads or X. But the user doesn't care where something came from — **they're
looking for interesting content and they want to keep what's useful.** borkmarkr
is the layer above all of them.

## The enemy: platform bookmarks are a write-only graveyard

This is the sharpest angle in the whole product, and it's worth leading with.
JP's words, lightly organised:

> "Clicking like might help the algorithm feed you more stuff, but their
> bookmarks aren't useful. People don't browse them, they don't review them,
> they don't look at how many times you look at something, you don't know the
> dates, you can't order them by date, you can't order them."

Everything you "save" on a platform goes into a bottomless, unsorted, unsearchable
list — and the platform has no incentive to fix it, because their business is
your *next* scroll, not your last one. You end up with thousands of saved posts
you will never see again.

Concrete contrasts that all land:

| Platform saves | borkmarkr |
|---|---|
| One flat list per app, 5+ apps | One library, all apps |
| No titles | Real titles and thumbnails |
| No categories | Auto-filed into 50 topics, ~612 subtopics |
| No search worth the name | Search titles, tags, notes, people — plus meaning |
| No dates, no ordering | Ordered by when you saved it |
| No idea what you revisit | Tracks what you actually go back to |
| Can't share a set | Share a curated collection as a link |

## Vocabulary

A saved item is a **brk** (plural **brks**). You *brk* something. "412 brks."
Own the word — it's what turns the name from a joke into a brand.

## The wedge: you already have thousands of these

The strongest acquisition story is not "start saving things". It's **"import the
1,400 things you already saved"**. Every platform is legally required to hand
over your data (X archive, Instagram's Download Your Information, TikTok export,
Google Takeout, Pinterest). Drop the file in and a dead list becomes a
categorised, searchable library in one step.

That before/after is the demo. *(Not built yet — see status below.)*

## Status — what is real today

Be careful here. Marketing that promises the roadmap will burn the launch.

**Working now:**
- Native iOS app, running on device
- Save any link; auto-filed into 50 topics / ~612 subtopics
- Two browse axes that cross — by Topic, or by Source (app it came from)
- Search across titles, tags, notes, people, plus a "Related" meaning-based strip
- Dated notes per brk
- Masonry feed of content-native cards; light/warm design, 4 accent themes
- Everything local and offline — nothing leaves the phone

**Built but blocked on a paid Apple account:**
- Share-extension saving (tap Share in any app → borkmarkr). This is the core
  acquisition path and it needs an App Group entitlement.

**Not built — do not market yet:**
- Accounts, cloud sync, sharing collections with people, the friend feed
- Bulk import
- Habit/mission tracking
- Real thumbnails are **partial**: YouTube and most web pages yes; Instagram,
  TikTok and X mostly no, because they don't publish metadata to unauthenticated
  requests. Don't show a mockup where every card has a rich Instagram thumbnail.

## Angles worth exploring

1. **"Your likes are a landfill."** Lead with the pain; everyone recognises it.
2. **"Where did I see that thing about…"** — the universal experience of knowing
   you saved something and never finding it again.
3. **Curated collections as the shareable artefact** — "my 40 best marathon-prep
   saves" is a thing people want to send. This is also the growth loop.
4. **Privacy** — it's local-first and offline. Genuinely differentiating against
   anything cloud-based, and true today.

## Tone

Playful, not precious. The name is silly on purpose. The product is not — it's
for people who save a lot and are quietly annoyed that they can never find any
of it. Avoid "productivity", avoid "second brain" (crowded, and it oversells
this); it's closer to *"the folder your feeds never gave you."*
